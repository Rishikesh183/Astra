# Demo video script: Physics Witness

Target length **about 2:45** (keep it under 3:00). 16:9, 1920×1080, 30 fps.

Each scene is one of two kinds:
- **MG**: motion graphics, generated by your tool from the description below.
- **REC**: a screen recording of the real app. Record these yourself from the deployed URL, at the browser's 100% zoom, with the mouse moving slowly.

## Visual style (for the motion graphics tool)

- **Look:** an accident investigation file. Dark asphalt background `#17191d`, with panels a step lighter (`#22252b`).
- **Accent:** hi-vis yellow `#ffd000`. A yellow/black hazard stripe as a divider and a transition wipe. A diagonal "CLAIM UNDER REVIEW · DO NOT CROSS · EVIDENCE" police tape that slides across between acts.
- **Verdict colours:**
  - Supported: green `#22c55e`
  - Contradicted: red `#ef4444`
  - Can't tell: amber `#f59e0b`
- **Type:** condensed bold capitals for headlines (Barlow Condensed or Oswald); a clean sans for body text (Inter); a monospace for timestamps (JetBrains Mono, as in `t=3.47s`).
- **Motion:** snappy, about 250 ms ease-out. Text types on like a report. Timestamps tick like a timecode. No 3D, no glow-heavy effects.
- **Logo:** a yellow warning triangle with an eye in it, and the wordmark "PHYSICS WITNESS".
- **Music:** low, steady, documentary/tension pulse. Duck it under the voiceover.

## Scene list

### 1. Hook (0:00–0:12) · MG
**Visual:** black screen. A dashcam frame fades in, slightly grainy, with a timecode in the corner ticking `00:00:02.80`. A typed quote appears over it in white: *"A train came from the left."* Freeze. A red stamp slams onto the quote: **CONTRADICTED · t=2.80s**.
**On-screen text:** "The story says one thing. The footage says another."
**Voiceover:** "Every accident claim comes with a story. More and more, it also comes with a video. But who checks that the two actually match?"

### 2. The problem (0:12–0:32) · MG
**Visual:** a stack of claim folders falls into frame. Each folder has a small video thumbnail and a page of text, and a counter climbs: 1, 10, 100, 1,000. An adjuster icon sits with a "watching" clock icon spinning. Then three small mismatch chips pop up, each pointing at a detail:
- "train: left or right?"
- "who moved first?"
- "was the light red?"

**On-screen text:** "Thousands of video claims. Every one watched by hand."
**Voiceover:** "Insurers and fleet operators get thousands of video claims. Someone has to watch each clip and compare it, sentence by sentence, with what the claimant wrote. It's slow, and small mismatches, like which side the other car came from, are easy to miss."

### 3. What it is (0:32–0:50) · MG
**Visual:** the logo builds in. On the left, a video tile. On the right, a story tile. Arrows from both meet in a central "Physics Witness" box. Out of it, three verdict chips stack up one by one: green **Supported**, red **Contradicted**, amber **Can't tell**, each with a monospace timestamp next to it.
**On-screen text:** "Video + story in → a verdict for every sentence, with the seconds that prove it."
**Voiceover:** "Physics Witness reads the footage like an accident investigator. You give it the video and the claimant's story. It checks every sentence and returns Supported, Contradicted, or Can't tell, each with the exact seconds of evidence."

### 4. Live demo: a sample claim (0:50–1:25) · REC
**Record:**
1. The home page with the claim cards (about 3 s).
2. Click **No video? Try a sample** and hover the three samples.
3. Pick **"Level crossing: the train came from the other side?"** (the liar sample).
4. The progress screen appears. If it's cached it finishes fast. Otherwise, cut the wait out.
5. The report:
   - The video on the left and the statements on the right.
   - Slowly move to the red **Contradicted** row: "A train came from the left."
   - Click it, and the video jumps to the evidence frame showing the train coming from the right.
   - Open "What the model saw" or the reasoning text for 2 seconds.
6. Scroll past the green Supported rows.

**Overlay (MG on top of the recording):** a yellow circle around the Contradicted row, and a callout: "Jumps to t=2.80s".
**Voiceover:** "Here's a real level-crossing clip. The claimant says the train came from the left. Physics Witness splits the story into statements and checks each one against the footage. Everything else holds up, but this one doesn't. The train clearly arrives from the right. One click jumps straight to the frame that shows it."

### 5. Can't tell → ask for evidence (1:25–1:45) · REC + MG
**Record:** a claim with a **Can't tell** statement (for example, the weather, or the intersection sample after the dashcam is knocked skyward). Show the "Ask the claimant" card and its specific request, then the button to upload the extra clip.
**MG insert (3 s):** an amber chip turns into an envelope icon with the text "Please send the rear camera angle, 10 s after impact". A new clip arrives, and the chip flips to green.
**Voiceover:** "When the footage can't settle a statement, it doesn't guess. It says Can't tell, and writes the claimant one specific request, like the rear camera or the ten seconds after impact. Upload that clip, and only the open statements are re-judged."

### 6. How it works (1:45–2:15) · MG
**Visual:** a left-to-right pipeline diagram that builds node by node. Each node lights up yellow as the voiceover reaches it.
1. **Video** → *Frame sampler* (a film strip; frames get a `t=` timestamp band burned in, and high-motion frames are highlighted)
2. → **NVIDIA Nemotron VL** (vision): "what physically happened, and when" (event chips with times: "train enters from right · 2.1s", "impact · 2.8s", "camera jolt · 3.4s")
3. **Story** → **Nemotron Nano**: splits it into atomic statements
4. Both feed **Nemotron Ultra**, the judge. A gavel icon, with the rule text: "Contradicted needs positive evidence. Missing footage = Can't tell."
5. Side input: **Tavily**, the weather and place on the claim date (a cloud icon)
6. Footer bar: "All models via **Nebius Token Factory** · App on **Nebius Serverless** · Claims in **Supabase**"

**Voiceover:** "Under the hood, frames are sampled where the motion is, and each is stamped with its true time. An NVIDIA Nemotron vision model reports only what physically happened and when. Nemotron Nano breaks the story into checkable statements, and Nemotron Ultra cross-examines each one. Its rules: no Contradicted without positive evidence, and missing footage means Can't tell. Every model runs on Nebius Token Factory."

### 7. Real-world impact (2:15–2:35) · MG
**Visual:** four role cards slide in, in a 2×2 grid, each with an icon and a one-line benefit:
- **Claims adjusters**: "Watch the flagged seconds, not the whole clip."
- **Fraud teams**: "Evidence, not accusations."
- **Fleet operators**: "Check the driver's report against the dashcam."
- **Claimants**: "Prove the footage backs your story."

Then a banner across the bottom: **"Evidence for a human, never a fraud verdict."**
**Voiceover:** "Adjusters only watch the seconds that matter. Fraud teams get evidence, not accusations. Fleets can check a driver's report against the dashcam, and honest claimants can prove their story. Physics Witness gives evidence to a human. It never decides fraud on its own."

## Recording tips

- Record the REC scenes on the **deployed** site, after the samples are cached, so nothing waits on camera.
- Before you record the liar sample, check that its live result really shows "A train came from the left." as **Contradicted**. If it doesn't, change the voiceover in scene 4 to match what the screen shows. Never overlay a verdict the app didn't give.
- Hide bookmarks and extensions, and use a clean browser profile.
- The sample footage carries a broadcaster logo (Rossiya 1), and the Dallas clip comes from a compilation. Keep them as short on-screen excerpts. If you can, use the public-domain Florida clip for scene 5.
